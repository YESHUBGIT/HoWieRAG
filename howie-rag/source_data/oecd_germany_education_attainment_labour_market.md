# Germany: educational attainment and labour-market outcomes

In Germany, family background remains associated with tertiary attainment. Among young adults whose parents had not completed upper secondary education, approximately one in five attained a tertiary qualification. Among young adults with at least one tertiary-educated parent, approximately three in five attained a tertiary qualification. The exact figure is subject to statistical uncertainty because of small sample size.

For adults aged 25 to 64 in Germany, unemployment rates were nearly the same for tertiary-educated adults and adults with upper secondary attainment: 2.5% for tertiary-educated adults and 2.6% for those with upper secondary attainment.

The main labour-market benefit of tertiary education in Germany is earnings. Adults aged 25 to 64 with a tertiary qualification earned on average 50% more than those with upper secondary education. The OECD average earnings advantage was 54%.
